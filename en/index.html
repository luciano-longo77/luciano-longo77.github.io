About Me

Scholarly Profile

I am an independent researcher specializing in authorial philology, genetic criticism and the digital representation of texts. I study the genesis of literary works through manuscripts, typescripts, redactional layers and interoperable encoding models based on TEI/XML.

I was trained at the University of Palermo, where I earned a PhD in Literary, Philological and Linguistic Studies with a digital critical edition of Attilio Bertolucci’s unpublished poems from the 1930s. This research gave rise to a lasting interest in compositional processes, in the forms of the unfinished and in authorial hesitations in modern texts. I completed advanced training in textual criticism at the Summer School of the University of Ferrara (dir. Paolo Trovato) and have taught courses in Contemporary Italian Literature, Digital Humanities and Theory and Methods of the Digital Edition at the University of Palermo and at LUMSA.

I have published contributions in the field of digital scholarly editing (Dai testi cartacei ai testi virtuali, Umanistica Digitale, 2018 ), Vittorinian genetic criticism (Molteplicità testuale e movimenti compositivi nel ms. di “Populonia”, 2018, ) and computational analysis applied to contemporary narrative (Esperimento su Elena Ferrante, 2019 ). In addition, I have collaborated on the Lexicon of Scholarly Editing (ESTS ) with entries devoted to the key concepts of genetic criticism ( ).

Alongside my research activity, I work in the field of digital processes in healthcare as District Leader – Service Manager at Dedalus S.p.A. , coordinating teams and complex architectures. This experience, combined with a Master's in management of healthcare organizations earned at SDA Bocconi, reinforces my interest in the governance of data, the modelling of information flows and the design of workflows for the Digital Humanities.

My approach combines philology, digital technologies and a systemic vision, with the aim of building models for the representation of the text that are rigorous, readable and able to render the complexity of creative processes.

The data, the TEI/XML models and the workflows that support this research activity are documented in versioned repositories, used as the primary locus of methodological implementation ( Research repository ).

Attilio Bertolucci

The project presents the genetic digital scholarly edition of the poetic working notebooks (scartafacci) of Attilio Bertolucci, consisting of 63 authorial documents (manuscripts and typescripts) held at the Archivio di Stato di Parma (Fondo Bertolucci, Busta II, fasc. 4), datable between 1930 and 1937 and attributable to the formative phase of his poetic work.

The materials include drafts, working copies, texts with stratified corrections, multiple revisions, and authorial notes. The edition treats writing as an ongoing process rather than a stabilized product, formally addressing the unfinished nature of these texts, in the tradition of authorial philology and of Contini's criticism of the scartafacci.

From an editorial and computational standpoint, the corpus is entirely encoded in TEI‑XML P5. The architecture of the genetic apparatus adopts the method of parallel segmentation, declared in <variantEncoding>, and uses exclusively the pair <app>/<rdg>, without recourse to <lem>, in order to represent the coexistence of the different textual virtualities without lemmatic hierarchization.

The redactional phases are modelled as concurrent textual layers (tb0, tb1, T1, T2, critical text) and are related to one another through an internal apparatus that records immediate and late variants, wholesale rewritings, deletions, additions and reprises.

Particular attention is devoted to the material dimension of the writing. The TEI markup includes the codicological description of the sources (<msDesc>), the identification of the hands and writing implements (<handNotes>), as well as the fine-grained marking of graphic and corrective phenomena by means of <add>, <del>, <space>, <gap>, <unclear> and <supplied>.

The edition further integrates the systematic linking of text and image through the <facsimile> module, allowing the alignment between textual units and digital reproductions of the autographs by means of <graphic> and <pb>.

The GitHub repository constitutes the formal home of the editorial project and documents the entire workflow: complete TEI files, structure of the genetic apparatus, encoding criteria, organization of the sources and methodological declarations contained in the <teiHeader>. The repository is conceived as an open resource for the study of Bertolucci's writing and as a model of digital genetic edition applicable to other authorial corpora.

Elio Vittorini

The Manoscritto di Populonia is an unfinished novel project developed by Elio Vittorini between 1959 and 1961, preserved as fragments across an extensive collection of manuscript and typescript papers. The editorial tradition has handed down a stabilized text on the basis of the posthumous editions of the 1970s, but without a genuinely systematic collation of the authorial sources.

The research takes as its starting point the study of the manuscript sources and from the central role of the red typescript (dsR), the only materially verifiable source bearing authorial interventions, never taken as the editorial pivot in the printed tradition. The analysis calls into question the primacy attributed to a typescript that is today untraceable (dsN*), highlighting the distance between authorial will and editorial will.

The article “Molteplicità testuale e movimenti compositivi nel Manoscritto di Populonia”, published in 2018 in Umanistica Digitale, constitutes the first formal outcome of the research. In it a genetic-documentary reconstruction of the fragment is proposed, showing how the text should be interpreted not as a stabilized product, but as a narrative process in the making, traversed by rewritings, blank spaces and alternative nuclei.

Starting from these premises, the ongoing study develops the hypothesis of a genetic digital scholarly edition of the Manoscritto di Populonia, founded on the stratigraphic reconstruction of the writing. The project takes the manuscript as the primary site of narrative planning and interprets the incompleteness not as an accident, but as a structural form of the text.

From a methodological standpoint, the digital edition is based on XML‑TEI encoding P5 and on a genetic-variantist model oriented toward the representation of textual plurality. The digital environment makes it possible to display the different competing textualities and to render the compositional dynamics of the narrative project, placing the reader before a mobile and non-teleological textuality.

The case of Populonia is approached as a critical laboratory for the study of unfinished narrative of the second half of the twentieth century, in dialogue with Vittorini's theoretical reflection on the concept of design, of the modern city and of writing as an exploratory activity, as it also emerges from contemporary texts and from the reflections collected in Le due tensioni.

Article (Umanistica Digitale)

Seventeenth-Century Mystical Texts

This section gathers projects dedicated to seventeenth-century mystical writings produced in contexts of doctrinal scrutiny, in particular female and lay-tertiary autograph texts characterized by glosses, quotations and corrective declarations inserted as devices of theological self-justification.

The common element of the corpus is not the devotional genre, but the presence of a functional and regulative intertextuality: biblical quotations, liturgical references, Teresian formulas and metatextual glosses that intervene at the points of maximum doctrinal exposure, operating as semantic enclosures within an inquisitorial or para-inquisitorial regime.

The project takes as its founding case Il Castello dell'anima by Sister Teresa di San Geronimo (Palermo, late seventeenth century), a unique manuscript preserved at the Biblioteca Comunale di Palermo. In this text mystical writing takes shape as a closely scrutinized process: the authorial voice proceeds by advances and immediate rebalancings, entrusted to glosses, explicit declarations and stabilizing quotations.

From a methodological standpoint, the section develops an integrated interpretive TEI + controlled AI model, designed to make observable and measurable functions traditionally described only in qualitative terms. TEI annotation formalizes intertextual occurrences as functional units, endowed with attributes for rhetorical function, risk class and impact index.

The AI component operates exclusively in expert-in-the-loop mode: it does not interpret the text, but generates controlled counterfactual variants (removal of glosses, relocation of quotations, simulated additions), on which it is possible to test in a replicable manner their effects on argumentative clarity, local cohesion and perceived doctrinal stability.

The model is conceived as transferable: not exclusive to the Castello dell'anima, but applicable to analogous corpora of mysticism, asceticism and spiritual direction in the post-Tridentine age, allowing comparisons grounded on common units and metrics.

Research Article • Umanistica Digitale

From Paper Texts to Virtual Texts: The Potentialities of Digital Textual Criticism Dai testi cartacei ai testi virtuali: potenzialità dell’ecdotica digitale

An approach to the encoding of authorial manuscripts using the TEI standards to represent the genetic phases of the creative process.

This paper introduces potentialities and criticisms of digital textual criticism. The aim is to shed light on the importance of the activities related to text reconstruction with regard to the type of text to be edited. Three types of texts are first analyzed and a markup model is proposed for each of them, namely: the last will of an author, an unpublished text, and preliminary texts of a literary work never completed. The exemplar texts here discussed are: Attilio Bertolucci's autographs (belonging to the 30s-40s period); an excerpt from the original manuscript 2Qq E 29 (late 17th century) by Teresa di S. Geronimo, and the “manoscritto di Populonia” (1959-1961) by Elio Vittorini.

Article • Edizioni ETS, 2018

Textual Multiplicity and Compositional Movements in the “ms. di Populonia”: Hypothesis for a Digital Project Molteplicità testuale e movimenti compositivi nel “ms. di Populonia”: ipotesi di un lavoro digitale

The paper offers an analysis of the ms. of Populonia as an object of textual multiplicity, reconstructable only on the basis of the dynamics of its compositional movements. Through the study of the variants, the rewritings and the internal dislocations of the manuscript, the contribution shows how the text does not answer to a stabilized form, but to a processual and stratified logic, characteristic of Vittorini's writing. On this basis, the hypothesis is advanced of a digital undertaking that, by means of encoding and modelling tools, would make it possible to represent the genetic complexity of the manuscript, rendering the plurality of textual paths and compositional phases without reducing them to a linear sequence.

2015 Reviewed
Monograph - Book chapter • Edizione Dell’Orso

Sister Teresa di San Geronimo’s Il Castello dell’anima: Critical Edition

Critical edition of "Libro III" (Ms. 2 Qq E 29 – late 17th cent. – of the Biblioteca Comunale di Palermo)

Il Castello dell’anima is a treatise on silent prayer, hitherto unpublished, preserved in autograph form in ms. 2 Qq E 29 of the Biblioteca Comunale di Palermo (late 17th cent.). Its author, the Carmelite tertiary Sister Teresa di San Geronimo, born Anna La Longa, wrote it drawing inspiration from the great Carmelite mystics of the 17th century: Saint Teresa de Ávila and Saint Juan de la Cruz. The treatise, however, also reflects the Quietist approach of the Guía espiritual (1675), written by the Spanish priest Miguel de Molinos. After the condemnation of Molinos, from the mid-1680s onward, anyone who was suspected of Quietism was persecuted by the Inquisition. Sister Teresa too was caught up in the accusations and the trials for Quietism that took place in Palermo between the end of the seventeenth and the early eighteenth century. The Carmelite tertiary was subjected to silence and enclosure, so that she might atone for her attempt to carve out, wholly independently, a space of intellectual freedom, in order to escape the two age-old “vocations” imposed on women: marriage and the convent. Il Castello dell’anima is the only surviving work of Sister Teresa.
